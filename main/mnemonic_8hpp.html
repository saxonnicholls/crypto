Detailed Description

bip39::mnemonic: entropy to words and back with the checksum, canonical text, and the BIP-39 seed.

Overview

A BIP-39 mnemonic is 12, 15, 18, 21 or 24 words from bip39::english that encode 16 to 32 bytes of entropy and a checksum of 4 to 8 bits (the first bits of its SHA-256). This header converts between the three forms a wallet meets, and derives the 64-byte seed that BIP-32 and SLIP-10 start from:

  • from_entropy(bytes): entropy to a mnemonic (the checksum computed).
  • parse(text): canonical text to a mnemonic, the checksum verified.
  • mnemonic::to_text(buffer): the words, separated by single spaces, into a fixed buffer.
  • to_entropy(m): the entropy back, in a secret_bytes<32>.
  • to_seed(m, passphrase): PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA512 of the text, salt "mnemonic" + passphrase, 2048 rounds.

Canonical text only. parse accepts lower-case words a to z separated by exactly one ASCII space, with no space before the first word or after the last, and nothing else (errc::non_canonical). Wallets differ on what they forgive (upper case, several spaces, tabs, a trailing newline) and each forgiveness is a way for two programs to derive different seeds from what a person believes are the same words; a caller that wants to accept looser input normalises it first, visibly.

NFKD. BIP-39 hashes the NFKD normalisation of the mnemonic and of the passphrase. English mnemonics are ASCII, where NFKD changes nothing. A passphrase must therefore be ASCII (passphrase_form::ascii, the default), or the caller states that it has already normalised it to NFKD (passphrase_form::nfkd), and it is used byte for byte; a non-ASCII passphrase without that statement is refused with errc::non_canonical, never hashed as given, because another wallet would hash its NFKD form and the seed would silently differ. This library has no NFKD of its own: snicholls-core's Unicode tables (unreleased) carry canonical decomposition (NFD) only, and NFKD needs the compatibility mappings too. When core has them, to_seed can normalise itself.

One. The PBKDF2 is the library's interim one (detail/hashes.hpp) until snicholls-core releases its own.

Guarantees and contracts
  • Secrets: the entropy, the words, the text and the passphrase. Every path over them is constant time in their values: words are looked up by comparing against all 2048 entries with masks (never a search), parse scans the text with masks (no branch on a character, every word slot written on every space), to_text places each word with masks over the whole buffer, and the checksum is compared with masks.
  • Public: the number of words (it is the length of the entropy), the length of the text (parse sees it and to_text returns it), and which error a failure is. Lengths of individual words are not revealed by timing.
  • mnemonic wipes its word indices on destruction; copies only by clone(). No allocation anywhere.
  • Everything is constexpr except to_seed (it reads the passphrase's characters as bytes, which a constant expression cannot, and 2048 PBKDF2 rounds are no work for a compiler anyway).
Errors

from_entropy: errc::invalid_mnemonic_length unless 16, 20, 24, 28 or 32 bytes. parse, in this order: errc::invalid_mnemonic_length for empty or over-long text, errc::non_canonical, errc::invalid_mnemonic_length for a word count other than 12, 15, 18, 21 or 24, errc::unknown_word, errc::invalid_checksum. to_seed: errc::non_canonical for a non-ASCII passphrase given as passphrase_form::ascii.

Examples
const std::array<std::uint8_t, 16> entropy{0x7f, 0x7f, 0x7f, 0x7f, 0x7f, 0x7f, 0x7f, 0x7f,
0x7f, 0x7f, 0x7f, 0x7f, 0x7f, 0x7f, 0x7f, 0x7f};
auto words = bip39::from_entropy(entropy); // result<mnemonic, errc>: checksum appended
std::array<char, bip39::max_text_length> text{};
const std::size_t n = words->to_text(text); // "legal winner thank ... yellow", no allocation
auto parsed = bip39::parse(std::string_view(text.data(), n)); // canonical text only, checksum verified
const auto back = bip39::to_entropy(*parsed); // the 16 bytes again, in secret_bytes
const auto seed = bip39::to_seed(*parsed, "TREZOR"); // PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA512, 2048 rounds
const auto typo = bip39::parse("legal winner thank year wave sausage worth useful legal winner thank thank");
const auto shouted = bip39::parse("LEGAL winner thank year wave sausage worth useful legal winner thank yellow");
const auto accented = bip39::to_seed(*parsed, "caf\xc3\xa9"); // non-ASCII without the NFKD statement

Known issues and gotchas

to_text writes the words without a terminating NUL; it returns the length.

to_seed is constant time except in the length of the mnemonic's text: PBKDF2 hashes the text, and the number of SHA-512 blocks it takes (and whether the HMAC key is first hashed, above 128 bytes) follows the length, the sum of the word lengths. That length carries about four to five bits of the words (its spread over the 2048-word list), against 128 to 256 bits of entropy; the Valgrind check declassifies it explicitly. Hiding it would need a SHA-512 whose final blocks are chosen by masks over the maximum length.
